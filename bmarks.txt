> and perturbation was the 4 lane stuff right?  Is there room now for any SIMD?                                                                                                              

● Right — the "perturbation" column is the branch's final form: the shared-index glitch engine with the 4-lane scalar kernel (v4), adaptive job sizing, 32-row remote jobs. All of today's
  kernel-level tuning, in other words.

  Is there room for SIMD now? Mostly no — BLA closed the window, for a structural reason worth spelling out:

  1. Across pixels (the classic approach — what the 4-lane kernel did): BLA's control flow is per-pixel — each pixel takes different-length skips at different orbit indices and rebases at
  its own times. Vectorizing that means lock-stepping lanes to the shortest skip and gathering table entries from different addresses per lane — and neither wasm SIMD128 nor NEON has gather
  loads. You'd give back most of what BLA won. This is exactly the trade we evaluated when choosing rebasing+BLA over the lane engine, and BLA won 7–11x vs the lanes' 1.3x.
  2. Within a pixel (complex mul as f64x2): we measured this twice today in other guises — the shuffle/mask overhead of packing one complex number into a vector loses to scalar on your
  cores (NEON 0.78x, wasm 0.69x). The BLA loop is a latency chain, and SIMD doesn't shorten latency.
  3. The one place SIMD still legitimately lives: the HP reference orbit. It's now the only bulk arithmetic left — and it's already SIMD-accelerated in wasm (the simd32 SIMD128 limb kernels
  kick in at 11+ limbs; your 126-digit view uses 15, so they're active). With 32-row jobs the reference is only a few percent of each job, so even doubling its speed moves the needle ~2%.

  The honest big picture: the day's arc went "make the arithmetic parallel" (SIMD/ILP, ~1.3x) → "delete the arithmetic" (BLA, ~10x), and once the arithmetic is deleted there's little left
  for SIMD to chew on. The remaining levers are algorithmic or structural: floatexp to go past 300 digits, reference/table sharing across jobs (saves the last few percent of HP work), and
  whatever pipeline ceiling the 1700-rows/sec plateau turns out to be. On x86 hardware the within-pixel question could be re-asked (AVX2's cheap shuffles might make vectorized complex mul a
  small win), but that's a measurement for a machine you don't have.
